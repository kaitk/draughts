
Norway maple bark
MATERIAL SPECIFICATION • Acer platanoides • Blender Cycles
Asset context: mature Norway maple in an Estonian manor park; close-up trunk, branches and distant tree LODs.
1. Optical behaviour and anatomy
Treat mature bark as a predominantly opaque, rough biological dielectric. Microscopic light penetration and scattering are physically plausible, but explicit subsurface scattering (SSS) is not required for a convincing mature trunk. Ordinary diffuse reflection already approximates much unresolved scattering.
The species develops grey-brown, somewhat corky bark with elongated, narrow, intersecting ridges and furrows. Younger bark is smoother [1, 2]. The outer protective tissues include dead, suberized cork cells; bark is heterogeneous and is not equivalent to exposed wood [3, 4].
Studies of young woody stems demonstrate light penetration and wavelength-dependent absorption in stem tissues [5]. This supports an age-dependent optical treatment, but does not establish a scattering length or SSS weight for old Norway maple. Avoid extrapolating young-stem transmission to the mature trunk.
2. Surface construction
Use actual displacement or geometry for longitudinal ridges and intersecting fissures; use smaller displacement and normal maps for secondary cracking. Avoid exaggerated oak-like plates or pine-like scales.
Add fine, irregular cellular and fibrous bump; orient elongated texture along the local growth direction, including branch junctions and root flare.
Use desaturated grey-brown, warm grey and occasional olive-grey variation. Weathered ridges may be lighter than sheltered surfaces; avoid baked directional illumination and excessive AO.
Keep bark dry and matte, with weak broad dielectric highlights. Separate moss, lichens, exposed wood and moisture into their own masks or materials.
Priority: geometry and normal quality > albedo and roughness variation > optional microscopic SSS.

Recommended baseline: SSS weight = 0 for mature bark. Optional close-up test: weight 0.02, scale 0.0002 m (0.2 mm), RGB radius (1, 0.65, 0.4), assuming 1 Blender unit = 1 m and applied object scale. Radius and scale jointly control the scattering distance; RGB ratios are heuristic, not measured spectral data.
Do not use leaf-style translucency or backlighting on the trunk. Do not scatter light through the entire bark shell. Preserve deep fissure contrast and sharp silhouette structure. If a separate bark shell is used, ensure the geometry and underlying material do not permit unrealistic light leaks.
Blend material parameters using tissue maturity, local branch diameter and age attributes. Diameter is only a proxy for maturity. Fresh green shoots may justify more scattering than established woody branches; do not make all thin branches translucent.
4. Moisture and LOD
For damp bark, use a separate moisture mask to darken diffuse colour moderately and lower roughness. Do not increase SSS merely to simulate wetness. Disable explicit bark SSS on distant geometry and impostors; capture diffuse appearance, normals and relief instead.

5. Validation and acceptance criteria
Render identical camera and exposure settings with SSS disabled and enabled under overcast daylight, low-angle direct sunlight, strong backlighting and close-up grazing illumination. On old bark, the difference should be barely perceptible. Reject waxy, fleshy, rubbery or glowing bark; reject loss of crack contrast. Keep SSS disabled if no meaningful improvement is visible.
No species-specific visible-light scattering coefficients, calibrated IOR, roughness or scattering distances for mature Acer platanoides were established by the sources below. All numerical shader recommendations must therefore be treated as adjustable approximations.
