# 043: iOS

Status: in-progress

The app on iPhone and iPad through SDL on UIKit, drawing with SDL_GPU on Metal
([architecture](../docs/architecture/README.md): "Other platforms on SDL"). The GPU side is
proven on the Mac, which already draws through Metal; what is new is the target, the
toolchain, the bundle, signing, and the lifecycle. Built slowly, from the basics: a sample
in the simulator first, then the app in 2D, then 3D, then a device.

## Feasibility (2026-11-09)

- **Goose:** the release (v2026.4) has no iOS target and no cross-compiling, but needs
  none: as for Android, `goose -o` writes the program's C and `goose --emit-runtime` the
  runtime's, and Xcode's clang compiles them for `arm64-apple-ios` and
  `arm64-apple-ios-simulator`. The runtime's C has Apple branches already (the native
  stack's bounds through `pthread_get_stackaddr_np`, `mmap` reservations, `__thread`), and
  nothing iOS lacks (no `system`, `fork`, or `exec`).
- **Data stacks:** Goose reserves 2 GB of address space per data stack, and the app has 26
  (`goose build --stacks`: 18 for the program, 8 for the computer's worker), 52 GB. An iOS
  process may map far less without the extended virtual addressing entitlement, so the
  build passes `--stack-reserve 128M` (3.3 GB). The largest stacks hold a few MB (the
  computer's 4 MB table, a 16 MB image while decoded); a stack that outgrows 128 MB stops
  the program with Goose's error, so 3D and the computer's games must be tried on it.
- **SDL3:** its CMake builds for iOS with `CMAKE_SYSTEM_NAME=iOS` and the simulator's or
  the device's SDK, with Ninja; no Xcode project. Static, as goose_gfx links it on the
  desktop. `SDL_RunApp` starts `UIApplicationMain` and calls the program's main (compiled
  as `SDL_main`, as on Android) on the main thread once the app has launched.
- **goose_gfx:** its C layers compile unchanged with the same patched copy Android uses
  (`patches/goose_gfx/`), so iOS gets draw-on-demand and compressed textures too.
- **Metal:** SDL_GPU wants `MTLGPUFamilyApple3` (A9 and later, so every iPhone iOS 16
  runs on). The simulator's device answers no for it and for Mac2, so SDL_GPU refused the
  simulator; `patches/SDL/0005` accepts it there ([findings](../docs/goose-findings.md)).
- **The bundle:** made by `scripts/ios.py` without Xcode's build system: the executable, an
  Info.plist written by the script (an empty `UILaunchScreen`, without which iOS runs the
  app letterboxed), the launcher's name per language from `strings/<lang>.txt`. The
  simulator takes an ad hoc signature.
- **Signing a device build:** a development identity and a provisioning profile for the
  app's id, from a free Apple ID's personal team (Xcode → Settings → Accounts). Without
  an Xcode project the profile comes from building a stub project once with
  `xcodebuild -allowProvisioningUpdates`, or from Xcode; the script then signs the bundle
  with it. None of the team's or the device's ids go in git. A free team's profile lasts
  7 days.

## Risks

- The lifecycle: iOS kills an app that submits GPU work in the background. SDL sends
  `WILL_ENTER_BACKGROUND` and `DID_ENTER_BACKGROUND`; the loop must draw nothing from
  then until the foreground, and write the game on the first (iOS may kill it any time
  after). Android blocks the loop in SDL instead.
- Address space on the device with 26 stacks at 128 MB, and the 3D board's memory on a
  4 GB phone.
- The photographs: the device's GPU samples ASTC (every Apple GPU from the A8), so it
  ships the ASTC files only, as Android does; the simulator may not, and needs the JPEGs.
- Without an Xcode project, the icon needs `actool` run by hand on an asset catalog.

## The simulator (2026-11-09)

On an iPhone 13 mini simulator (iOS 18.3, 1080 × 2340), booted headless by
`scripts/ios.py`: home's first run, a game against the computer (its worker thread on
128 MB stacks), a clocked game, and the 3D board in Estonian draw as on the Mac, the
photograph shown (the simulator build keeps the JPEGs, in case its GPU takes no ASTC;
which path it took is unchecked). Sent to the background
by starting another app and brought back, the same process draws again, with no Metal
error; a run without flags writes `game-1.bin` as it goes to the background, in the
container's `Library/Application Support/kaitk/draughts/`. The bundle is 13.7 MB with
the JPEGs and the ASTC files both embedded. The app knows iOS from `draughts_platform`
(`src/app/launch.c`): full screen, the clocks paused and nothing drawn without focus,
the display's scale for the layout, and home without its way out.

## Rotation (2026-11-09)

A turn between portrait and landscape stretched the picture through UIKit's animation:
the frame drawn at the new size was scaled to each size the view's bounds passed through.
`patches/SDL/0006` shows it centred at its own size while the transition runs
([findings](../docs/goose-findings.md)); filmed in the simulator, home's board stays
square through the turn both ways. On the iPhone 13 mini it is for the user to turn.
